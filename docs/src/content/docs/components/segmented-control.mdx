## Notes

**For a choice you can see whole.** The theme, a list shown as rows or cards,
"all / mine / shared". The shape settles three things a
[Select](/dowel/components/select/) cannot: every option is on screen, the choice
is one press rather than two, and switching back and forth is the same press
again. kilna put a Select where a segment belongs — the theme in its settings —
and drew the segment by hand where it had one.

```tsx
<SegmentedControl aria-label={t('theme')} value={theme} onValueChange={setTheme}>
  <Segment value="light">{t('light')}</Segment>
  <Segment value="dark">{t('dark')}</Segment>
  <Segment value="system">{t('system')}</Segment>
</SegmentedControl>
```

**It is a radio group, not a row of toggles.** Exactly one option is ever
chosen, which is what a radio says and a toggle does not: a toggle group lets
the last pressed button go, and "no theme" is not a theme. Base UI's RadioGroup
underneath gives the rest — one stop on the Tab key, the arrows move and choose
at once, and a reader hears "Dark, radio button, 2 of 3". For filters that can
all be off, use a [ChipGroup](/dowel/components/chip/).

**The chosen segment is lifted, not filled.** A sunken track, and the chosen
option raised out of it on the raised surface in semibold — the mockup's `.seg`.
An accent fill reads as "press me", which the chosen option is the one thing you
need not. The weight carries the choice too, so it survives a screen where the
two greys look alike.

**It stands on the small control row.** Its height is `h-control-sm`, so it sits
level with the small buttons and fields beside it and follows `data-density` with
them.

**An icon alone needs a name.** A segment's words are its accessible name; a
segment that is only an icon takes `aria-label`.
